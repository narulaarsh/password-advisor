## Participant information sheet

- **What the study is about.** How the timing and wording of password warnings affect the choices people make.
- **What you will do.**
  1. Read a fictional profile.
  2. Create three fictional passwords for made-up accounts, responding to any feedback shown.
  3. Answer ten short questions about how easy the tool was to use.
- **What is recorded.** For each task: which version of the tool you saw, whether a warning appeared, whether you kept your password, edited it or accepted a suggestion, and how long you took. An automatic check also records whether a simple offline guessing program would recover the final made-up password. Your answers to the ten questions are recorded too. We do **not** record your name, contact details, any password you type or your IP address. You are identified only by a slot ID such as S014.
- **Risks.** We know of no risks beyond normal computer use. Please never type a password you use or have used for a real account.
- **Your rights.** Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time without giving a reason. You can withdraw your data until [date] by quoting your slot ID to [contact].
- **Storage.** Data are kept on [storage] for [period] and then deleted. Only summary results are published.
- **Contact.** [Investigator email]. For concerns about how the study is run, contact [ethics committee contact].

> Thank you. You saw one of four versions of the tool. They differ in whether feedback appeared before or after saving, and whether a safer alternative was offered.
