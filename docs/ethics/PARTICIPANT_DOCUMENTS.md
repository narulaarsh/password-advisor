## Participant information sheet

- **What the study is about.** How the timing and wording of password warnings affect the choices people make.
- **What you will do.**
  1. Enter your first name, surname, home city and birth year. These are used to check whether a password could be guessed from them, and are stored with your responses.
  2. Make up passwords for three new accounts, responding to any feedback shown. Never use a real password.
  3. Answer ten short questions about how easy the tool was to use.
- **What is recorded.** For each task: which version of the tool you saw, whether a warning appeared, whether you kept your password, edited it or accepted a suggestion, and how long you took. An automatic check also records whether a simple offline guessing program, given your details and your earlier passwords from the session, would recover the final made-up password. To check public breach lists, only a 5-character fragment of a scrambled version of each password is sent. Your answers to the ten questions are recorded too. **We also store your first name, surname, city and birth year, every password you type in the study (readable by the researchers), any password the tool suggests and the reasons it gave.** Because of this, you must make up completely new passwords. We do not record contact details or your IP address (the hosting provider may keep standard server logs). You are identified only by a slot ID such as S014.
- **Risks.** We know of no risks beyond normal computer use. Please never type a password you use or have used for a real account.
- **Your rights.** Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time without giving a reason. You can withdraw your data until [date] by quoting your slot ID to [contact].
- **Storage.** Data are kept on [storage] for [period] and then deleted. Only summary results are published.
- **Contact.** [Investigator email]. For concerns about how the study is run, contact [ethics committee contact].
